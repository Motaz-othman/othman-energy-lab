How the Low-Carbon Cooling Selector works

The Low-Carbon Cooling Selector finds the data center cooling system with the lowest carbon, and gives its yearly emissions of a data center cooling system in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent. It adds three sources: the electricity the cooling plant uses, the energy needed to supply its make-up water, and the refrigerant that leaks from the chillers each year.

Method

  • Electricity: annual cooling energy (MWh) × grid emission factor (t CO₂ per MWh).
  • Water: annual water use (m³) × energy to supply 1 m³ (kWh) × grid emission factor.
  • Refrigerant: chiller charge (kg) × leak rate per year × global warming potential (GWP) of the refrigerant.
  • In Compare systems, energy and water come from an 8,760-hour simulation with the city's measured weather, the same model as the Cooling Plant Selector. In My own numbers, you enter them.

Frequently asked questions

Which part of a cooling system's CO₂ is largest?

Electricity, in almost every case: usually 85–99% of the total. Refrigerant leakage can matter with high-GWP refrigerants such as R-134a, and water matters in the Gulf, where desalinated water takes about 3.5 kWh per m³.

Where do the grid factors come from?

US cities use the EPA eGRID2023 subregion rates; Sydney uses the Australian National Greenhouse Accounts factors (NSW 2025–26); other countries use Our World in Data / Ember, latest year. You can type your own factor, for example a green-power contract.

Does it include the IT equipment?

No. Only the cooling system: chillers, heat-rejection fans, pumps, CRAH, fan walls and CDUs. IT electricity and UPS losses are not included.
