How the Peak Shaving Tank works

The Peak Shaving Tank sizes a chilled-water thermal storage tank that covers the rare extreme hottest hours of a data center cooling plant, so the chillers and their electrical supply can be rated for a lower design temperature. It finds the lowest design temperature whose tank still recharges safely during the 12-hour night, and shows the cut in rated power, the tank volume and the recharge time.

Method

  • A heatwave of the hottest days is built from the ASHRAE extreme values and the site daily range, with full IT load by day and the night IT load from 19:00 to 07:00.
  • For each design temperature the plant is rated lower and the tank covers the shortfall; it must be full again before morning, using only the capacity the night load leaves free.
  • The recommendation is the lowest design temperature whose tank recharges within the safe time (4 hours by default), checked against the ASHRAE 0.4% level.
  • The plant model is the same as the Cooling Plant Selector: ASHRAE 90.1-2025 chillers, approaches and pumps.

Frequently asked questions

How much can a chilled-water tank cut the cooling plant rated power?

Across the 33 built-in cities, typically 10–17% of the cooling plant rated power for air-cooled chillers, depending on how short and sharp the hottest hours are.

Why does recharge time matter?

After the hottest afternoon the tank must be full again before the next hot day. If it needs more than about 4 hours at the night load, recharging may not be safe during a heatwave: raise the design temperature or add a standby chiller.

Is this the same as a ride-through tank?

No. A ride-through tank covers a few minutes of full cooling during a power changeover. A peak shaving tank covers the extreme-weather hours and lets the plant be rated lower. A reserve can be kept full for ride-through.
