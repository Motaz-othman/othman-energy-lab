How the Liquid Rack Cooling works

Liquid Rack Cooling shows what a liquid-cooled GPU or CPU rack needs: the maximum coolant supply temperature, the cooling and flow per rack, the safe minimum flow for variable-flow control, the minimum buffer tank on the CDU loop, and how the CDU pump and control valve respond to a load jump or a pump trip. Platforms include NVIDIA GB300 and GB200 NVL72, Vera Rubin, HGX B200, AMD Instinct MI355X, Helios and MI300A, and Lenovo Neptune CPU racks.

Method

  • Required flow = heat to liquid ÷ (ρ × cp × (maximum return − supply)), which reproduces published OEM flow tables; the operating flow adds a margin (20% by default).
  • A load jump from idle to rated power is modelled with two water masses: the coolant at the chips and the CDU loop, with the CDU sensing delay, VFD pump ramp and control valve stroke.
  • The minimum tank is the smallest volume that keeps the return within its limit through the response gap at full flow.

Frequently asked questions

What coolant flow does an NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 rack need?

About 59 L/min at 25 °C supply up to 177 L/min at 45 °C supply, holding the return at about 55 °C (Lenovo GB300 NVL72 data), plus the operating margin.

What is the safe minimum flow at idle?

The lowest idle flow from which a jump to rated power does not push the coolant return past its limit before the VFD pump ramps up; never below the vendor minimum flow.

Do I need a buffer tank on the CDU loop?

Only if the water in the CDU and its pipes cannot cover the time the control valve and pump take to respond. Beyond that minimum, a bigger tank only lowers the supply temperature rise.

What happens if the CDU pump trips?

The racks hold very little heat: the coolant reaches its limit within seconds, so the standby CDU pump should run in parallel with automatic changeover.
