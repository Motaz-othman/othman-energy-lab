Method and equations

  • Weather: built-in cities use a measured typical year (TMYx 2011–2025) from the main airport station and ASHRAE 2025 Handbook of Fundamentals design conditions, both from climate.onebuilding.org. Hourly wet-bulb is limited to the ASHRAE extreme annual wet-bulb + 1 K. Psychrometrics per ASHRAE Fundamentals, Ch. 1.
  • Design cases: Case A = extreme dry-bulb + the wet-bulb of the hottest hour; Case B = extreme wet-bulb + the dry-bulb of the most humid hour that was not capped. Every system is checked at both at 100% load. The design peak is the highest of Case A, Case B and the typical-year maximum at 100% load, so no hour in the weather file exceeds it.
  • Loads: chiller load = IT load + CDU pumps or FWU fans + primary and secondary pumps. Pumps and fans: P = Q·ΔP / (ρ·cp·ΔT·η); VFD part load P = Pfull·(Q/Qdesign)³, minimum speed 50% pumps, 30% fans.
  • Free cooling = (Treturn − Tout) / (Treturn − Tsupply), 0–1. Tout: towers wb + 5.56 + 1.5 (plate HX); dry coolers db + 5.56 (direct on the loop); adiabatic above 24 °C: min(db + 5.56, Tpad + 9.4) + 1.5, with Tpad = db − 0.85 (db − wb); the pads run only when they give the colder water; db + 5.56 + 1.5 below 24 °C; hybrid: dry coolers alone up to 24 °C, towers above with the dry coolers in series ahead: in free-cooling hours dry share = (Tin − Tdry,out) / (Tin − Treq), Tin = return − 1.5, Treq = max(supply − 1.5, wb + 5.56), Tdry,out = max(db + 5.56, Treq); with chillers only (wb + 5.56 − db) / 5.56. Approaches: ASHRAE 90.1-2025 Table 6.8.1-7 rating points (minimum efficiency, not design selections).
  • Chillers: COP = COPrated × [(Te + 273.15)/(Tc − Te)] ÷ [(Te,ref + 273.15)/(Tc,ref − Te,ref)], limited to 14 (provisional, pending manufacturer low-lift data). Te = supply − 2; Tc = db + 15 (air-cooled) or condenser water + 7 (water-cooled). Rated COP: ASHRAE 90.1-2025 Path B (2.866 air-cooled, 6.018 water-cooled) at AHRI 551/591 (Te,ref 5 °C; Tc,ref 50 / 37 °C). Part load: PLF = a + b·PLR + c·PLR² = k × EnergyPlus variable-speed shape + (1 − k), k solved so the AHRI points, each at its own condenser temperature, reproduce the Path B IPLV (k = 0.867 water-cooled, 0.890 air-cooled; coefficients 0.1969 / 0.1318 / 0.6713 and 0.1752 / 0.1354 / 0.6895). The temperature benefit sits only in the Carnot term. Units staged by load, the next above 90% PLR. Air-cooled capacity −1.35% per K above 35 °C.
  • Heat rejection: Qr = chiller load + chiller power. Fans at full speed: towers 1.2%, dry and adiabatic coolers 3% of the heat (ASHRAE 2025 minimum / public data sheets); condenser pumps 211 kPa on 5.56 K. Condenser water above 43 °C gives a warning (a chiller rated above 43 °C is needed).
  • Water: towers 0.0020 × flow × range evaporation (EnergyPlus default 0.2 %/K; band 0.15–0.27 %/K) plus blowdown at CoC 4 = 2.3 L/h per kW rejected (1.7–3.1); hybrid on the towers' share; adiabatic Qr × (db − Tpad) / (12 × 2,430) × 1.15 in wet-mode hours only.
  • MLC (ASHRAE 90.4): annual mechanical energy ÷ annual IT energy; checked against ASHRAE 90.4-2025 Table 6.5 for the climate zone.
  • Electrical losses and PUE: PUE = (IT / ηUPS + cooling) / (1 − losses) / IT, UPS 96%, distribution 2%. Simplified, not the 90.4 ELC method.
  • Running cost: electricity × tariff + water × tariff + cooling peak × demand charge × 12. Simple payback against air-cooled chillers = extra initial cost (mid estimate) ÷ running cost saved.
  • Carbon (t CO₂e/yr): electricity × grid factor + water × supply energy (kWh/m³) × grid factor + refrigerant charge (duty + 1 standby per loop, kg/kW × capacity) × leak rate × GWP. Grid factors: EPA eGRID2023 rev 2 (US), Australian NGA (Sydney), Our World in Data / Ember (others); water energy: IEA WEO 2016 (Phoenix band to 1.6, CAP); GWP: IPCC AR4 via the EPA AIM Act, R-1234ze(E) EU 2024/573.
  • Recommendation: energy 4, water 3 (doubled when water is limited), peak 3, initial cost 2, carbon 3, normalised across feasible options. Energy and carbon are both scored on purpose: energy is the plant's efficiency, carbon its environmental impact, so electricity carries about half the weight. Full basis: the Cooling Plant Calculation Basis. Screening-level; confirm with vendor selections.

How the Cooling Plant Selector works

The Cooling Plant Selector compares five data center cooling plants hour by hour over a full weather year (8,760 hours) and recommends the right one for the site: air-cooled chillers, air-cooled chillers with free-cooling dry coolers, and water-cooled chillers with cooling towers, adiabatic coolers or hybrid dry coolers and towers, each with plate heat exchanger free cooling. It reports cooling electricity, annual PUE and ASHRAE 90.4 MLC, the electrical peak, water use, carbon emissions, running and initial cost, and a ranked recommendation.

Method

  • Weather: measured typical-year hourly data (TMYx 2011–2025) and ASHRAE 2025 design conditions for 33 built-in data center cities, or your own EPW / CSV file.
  • Two coincident design cases: the extreme dry-bulb with the wet-bulb of the hottest hour, and the extreme wet-bulb with the dry-bulb of the most humid hour. The design peak is the higher.
  • Chillers: ASHRAE 90.1-2025 Path B efficiency at AHRI 551/591 rating conditions, adjusted every hour for the actual lift, with VSD part-load curves calibrated to the ASHRAE IPLV and staging by load.
  • Free cooling = (return − water from the outdoor equipment) / (return − supply). Approaches from the ASHRAE 90.1-2025 heat-rejection rating points.
  • Pumps and fans from P = Q × ΔP / (ρ × cp × ΔT × η); carbon from local grid factors (EPA eGRID, Our World in Data), water supply energy and refrigerant leakage.

Frequently asked questions

Which cooling system is best for a data center in a hot climate such as Dubai or Riyadh?

For a liquid-cooled hall at 30/40 °C, water-cooled chillers with cooling towers and plate heat exchanger free cooling usually rank first: the lowest electricity, MLC and peak power. They use the most water, so where water is limited a hybrid dry cooler and tower plant or adiabatic coolers trade some energy for much less water. Air-cooled chillers have the highest electrical peak.

How is the ASHRAE 90.4 MLC checked?

The annual mechanical load component (cooling energy ÷ IT energy) is compared with the maximum annualized MLC in ASHRAE 90.4-2025 Table 6.5 for the site climate zone and IT size.

Why does a humid city use more cooling energy than a hotter dry one?

Cooling towers and their free cooling depend on the wet-bulb temperature, not the dry-bulb. A humid city such as Mumbai keeps a high wet-bulb all year, so it gets fewer free-cooling hours than Dubai with its dry winters.

How are the carbon emissions calculated?

Annual cooling electricity × the local grid emission factor, plus water × the energy to supply it (3.5 kWh/m³ for Gulf desalination) × the grid factor, plus refrigerant leakage × its GWP.
