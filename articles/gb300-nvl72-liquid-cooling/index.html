NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 liquid cooling: coolant flow, supply temperature and minimum flow

By Eng. Mutaz Othman · 22 November 2026

The NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 is a rack-scale system of 72 GPUs cooled directly by liquid. Designing the facility water and the CDU loop starts from what one rack needs.

Key figures per rack

ItemValue
Thermal design power135 kW, up to about 155 kW peak
Heat to liquidabout 90%; the rest to air
Coolant supply temperature25–45 °C (ASHRAE W45 class)
Coolant flowabout 59 L/min at 25 °C supply up to 177 L/min at 45 °C supply
Coolant returnheld at about 55 °C

Source: Lenovo GB300 NVL72 product guide.

Why the flow rises with supply temperature

The required flow follows the heat balance: flow = heat to liquid ÷ (density × specific heat × (maximum return − supply)). With the return held near 55 °C, a 45 °C supply leaves only a 10 K rise, so the flow is three times that at 25 °C. A warmer supply saves energy at the plant (more free cooling, lower chiller lift) at the cost of more pumping.

Minimum flow and the CDU response

Variable-flow control saves pump energy at idle, but the racks hold very little coolant. If the load jumps from idle to full power, the coolant heats up until the CDU pump ramps up and its control valve opens. The safe minimum flow is the lowest idle flow from which that jump does not push the return past its limit, and never below the vendor minimum. A small buffer tank on the CDU loop can cover the response gap; beyond that, a bigger tank only lowers the supply temperature rise.

Pump trip

If the CDU pump stops, the coolant reaches its limit within seconds. Run the standby CDU pump in parallel with automatic changeover, and keep the CDUs and facility pumps on UPS.
