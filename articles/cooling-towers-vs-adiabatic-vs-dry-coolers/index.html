Cooling towers vs adiabatic coolers vs dry coolers for data centers in hot climates

By Eng. Mutaz Othman · 22 November 2026

The heat-rejection equipment sets the condenser water temperature, and with it the chiller efficiency, the free-cooling hours and the electrical peak. In hot climates the choice is mainly between energy and water.

How close each one gets

EquipmentWater out at the ASHRAE 90.1-2025 rating pointDepends on
Cooling towerswet-bulb + 5.56 Kwet-bulb
Adiabatic coolers (pads on)wet-bulb + 11.1 Kwet-bulb, pads above about 24 °C
Dry coolersdry-bulb + 5.56 Kdry-bulb
Hybrid (dry coolers then towers in series)dry coolers alone in cool weather, towers aboveboth

A cooling tower cools water by evaporation, so it can reach close to the wet-bulb, which in a hot desert is 15–20 K below the dry-bulb. That is why towers give the lowest condenser water, the smallest chiller lift and the most free-cooling hours.

  • Towers win on energy, peak and carbon, but use the most water: in the Gulf that water is desalinated, which adds its own energy and carbon.
  • Hybrid keeps the tower peak and saves about 30% of the water in Dubai, more in milder climates.
  • Adiabatic coolers use less than half the water of towers, but their condenser water runs about 5.5 K warmer; on the most humid Gulf hours it can pass 43 °C, above the standard chiller range.
  • Dry coolers need no water, but in Dubai they free-cool only in the cooler months and the air-cooled chillers set a high peak.

Humidity matters more than heat

Towers and adiabatic coolers follow the wet-bulb. A humid city such as Mumbai, though cooler than Dubai, gets fewer free-cooling hours because its wet-bulb stays high all year. Compare any city in the Cooling Plant Selector.
