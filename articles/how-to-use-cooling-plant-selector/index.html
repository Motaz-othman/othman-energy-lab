How to use the Cooling Plant Selector

By Eng. Mutaz Othman · 22 November 2026

The Cooling Plant Selector compares five cooling plants for a data hall with a liquid cooling loop and an air cooling loop, hour by hour over a full weather year, and recommends the right one for the site. This guide walks through the inputs and how to read the results.

1. Choose the weather

Pick one of the 33 built-in data center cities in the City tab: Gulf cities (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Manama), major US markets (Northern Virginia, Dallas, Phoenix, Atlanta, Columbus, Silicon Valley, Hillsboro, Reno and others), Europe and Asia-Pacific. Each city loads its airport station's measured typical year (TMYx 2011–2025, 8,760 hours) and its ASHRAE 2025 design conditions. For another site, use the Weather file tab to upload an EPW file or a CSV with hourly dry-bulb and humidity; a sample CSV shows the format.

2. Enter the data hall load

  • Cooling load (MW): the IT load at full output.
  • Frequent load (%): the load the hall runs at most of the time; annual energy, water and cost use it. Peaks always use 100%.
  • Liquid cooling loop: its share of the load and its supply / return temperatures (default 30 / 40 °C for direct-to-chip cooling).
  • Air cooling loop: supply / return for the fan wall units (default 20 / 30 °C).

3. Set the site conditions

Water availability can be Available, Limited (water counts double in the ranking) or None (systems that need water are excluded). The design dry-bulb and wet-bulb are blank by default: the tool then checks two coincident design cases from the ASHRAE extremes, Case A with the extreme dry-bulb and Case B with the extreme wet-bulb. Enter a stricter value, such as an ASHRAE 20-year return period, to replace the extreme.

4. Adjust the engineering inputs (optional)

The collapsed sections hold the full basis, all with standard defaults you can override: utility tariffs; chillers (ASHRAE 90.1-2025 Path B COP, unit sizes, the condenser-water warning limit); heat rejection (ASHRAE approaches, fan power); pump heads and fan wall units; environmental impact (grid emission factor, refrigerant); electrical losses; and initial cost rates.

5. Read the results

The top of the results shows the two design cases and one card per system, the recommended one first: electricity, annual PUE, electrical peak, water, carbon, running and initial cost, with notes on free-cooling hours, savings against air-cooled chillers and the ASHRAE 90.4 MLC check. The tabs go deeper:

  • Overview: all options side by side and the design-day check at both cases, with the number of chillers.
  • Systems: the schematic of each plant with every pump, its equipment, operating modes and monthly free-cooling hours.
  • Energy & water: MLC against ASHRAE 90.4-2025, carbon by source, and electricity and water by component.
  • Cost: running cost, payback against air-cooled chillers, and the initial cost range.
  • Climate and Method: free-cooling potential by month, and every equation and source.

6. Export the report

PDF report opens a print-ready A4 report (save it with the browser's Save as PDF). Excel tables downloads every results table as a workbook.

Cooling towers rank first: half the electrical peak of air-cooled chillers and about half the carbon, at the cost of the most water. Where water is limited, the hybrid plant saves about 30% of the water for 7% more electricity.
