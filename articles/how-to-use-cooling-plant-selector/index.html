How to use the Cooling Plant Selector

By Eng. Mutaz Othman · 22 November 2026

The Cooling Plant Selector compares five cooling plants for a data hall with a liquid cooling loop and an air cooling loop, hour by hour over a full weather year (8,760 hours): air-cooled chillers, air-cooled chillers with free-cooling dry coolers, and water-cooled chillers with cooling towers, adiabatic coolers or hybrid dry coolers and towers, each with plate heat exchanger free cooling. It reports cooling electricity, annual PUE and ASHRAE 90.4 MLC, the electrical peak, water use, carbon emissions, and running and initial cost, and recommends one system for each water case: TSE available, water limited and no water. This guide walks through the inputs and how to read the results.

1. Choose the weather

Pick one of the 33 built-in data center cities in the City tab: Gulf cities (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Manama), major US markets (Northern Virginia, Dallas, Phoenix, Atlanta, Columbus, Silicon Valley, Hillsboro, Reno and others), Europe and Asia-Pacific. Each city loads its airport station's measured typical year (TMYx 2011–2025, 8,760 hours) and its ASHRAE 2025 design conditions. For another site, use the Weather file tab to upload an EPW file or a CSV with hourly dry-bulb and humidity; a sample CSV shows the format.

2. Enter the data hall load

  • Cooling load (MW): the IT load at full output.
  • Frequent load (%): the load the hall runs at most of the time; annual energy, water and cost use it. Peaks always use 100%.
  • Liquid cooling loop: its share of the load and its supply / return temperatures (default 30 / 40 °C for direct-to-chip cooling).
  • Air cooling loop: supply / return for the fan wall units (default 20 / 30 °C).

3. Set the design condition (optional)

The design dry-bulb and wet-bulb are blank by default: the tool then checks two coincident design cases from the ASHRAE extremes, Case A with the extreme dry-bulb and Case B with the extreme wet-bulb. Enter a stricter value, such as an ASHRAE 20-year return period, to replace the extreme.

4. Adjust the engineering inputs (optional)

The collapsed sections hold the full basis, all with standard defaults you can override: utility tariffs; chillers (ASHRAE 90.1-2025 Path B COP, unit sizes, the condenser-water warning limit); heat rejection (ASHRAE approaches, fan power); pump heads and fan wall units; environmental impact (grid emission factor, refrigerant); electrical losses; and initial cost rates.

5. Read the results

One card per system shows annual electricity, peak power, water and CO₂, with the best value in each row marked. Three cards carry a recommendation, one for each water case: If TSE is available (energy, water, peak power, initial cost and carbon weighed together), If water is limited (water counts double) and If there is no water (only dry systems). The tabs below the cards:

  • Systems: the schematic of each plant and its operation by month (free cooling, partial, chiller only) on each loop.
  • Energy & carbon: annual PUE, the ASHRAE 90.4 MLC check and the facility peak, with electricity by component and CO₂ by source.
  • Cost: running cost, initial cost range and payback against air-cooled chillers.
  • Climate: free-cooling potential of the site by month, for dry coolers and for cooling towers.

6. Export the report

PDF report opens a print-ready A4 report (save it with the browser's Save as PDF). Excel tables downloads the results, the recommendations, your inputs and the monthly operating hours as a workbook.

Frequently asked questions

Which cooling system is best for a data center in a hot climate such as Dubai or Riyadh?

For a liquid-cooled hall at 30/40 °C, water-cooled chillers with cooling towers and plate heat exchanger free cooling usually rank first: the lowest electricity, MLC and peak power. They use the most water, so where water is limited a hybrid dry cooler and tower plant or adiabatic coolers trade some energy for much less water. Air-cooled chillers have the highest electrical peak.

How is the ASHRAE 90.4 MLC checked?

The annual mechanical load component (cooling energy ÷ IT energy) is compared with the maximum annualized MLC in ASHRAE 90.4-2025 Table 6.5 for the site climate zone and IT size.

Why does a humid city use more cooling energy than a hotter dry one?

Cooling towers and their free cooling depend on the wet-bulb temperature, not the dry-bulb. A humid city such as Mumbai keeps a high wet-bulb all year, so it gets fewer free-cooling hours than Dubai with its dry winters.

How are the carbon emissions calculated?

Annual cooling electricity × the local grid emission factor, plus water × the energy to supply it (3.5 kWh/m³ for Gulf desalination) × the grid factor, plus refrigerant leakage × its GWP.
