How to use the Peak Shaving Tank

By Eng. Mutaz Othman · 24 November 2026

A cooling plant is rated for the extreme hottest hour at full IT load, a condition that rarely happens. The Peak Shaving Tank sizes a chilled-water tank that covers those few hours, so the chillers and their electrical supply can be rated lower. The tank recharges during the night.

1. Choose the weather

Pick one of the built-in cities, which load the measured hourly year and the ASHRAE design conditions of the main airport, or upload an EPW or CSV weather file.

2. Enter the load and plant

  • Cooling load: the chiller capacity at full IT load.
  • Night IT load: the average IT load over the 12-hour night, 19:00 to 07:00. The lower it is, the faster the tank recharges.
  • Cooling system: air-cooled systems, whose power peaks hardest in the hottest hours.
  • Supply and return: the chilled-water temperatures, which set how much cooling each cubic metre stores.

3. Set the design temperature

The tool fills in the recommended design temperature: the lowest one whose tank still recharges safely after the hottest afternoon. Type your own value to test it, or add a margin under the safety settings.

4. Read the results

  • Rated power cut: the reduction in the cooling plant's electrical rating, against the plant rated for the extreme.
  • Tank: the volume and the tank size.
  • Recharge time at the night load, and the hours per year the tank works.
  • The hottest-day chart shows the plant power with and without the tank, and the tank level through the day and night.
  • A reserve is kept full: it is never used for peak shaving and covers a power changeover or weather hotter than the design.
  • The typical-year check confirms the tank covers every hour of the measured year.

Frequently asked questions

How much can a chilled-water tank cut the cooling plant rated power?

Across the 33 built-in cities, typically 10–17% of the cooling plant rated power for air-cooled chillers, depending on how short and sharp the hottest hours are.

Why does recharge time matter?

After the hottest afternoon the tank must be full again before the next hot day. If it needs more than about 4 hours at the night load, recharging may not be safe during a heatwave: raise the design temperature or add a standby chiller.

Is this the same as a ride-through tank?

No. A ride-through tank covers a few minutes of full cooling during a power changeover. A peak shaving tank covers the extreme-weather hours and lets the plant be rated lower. A reserve can be kept full for ride-through.
